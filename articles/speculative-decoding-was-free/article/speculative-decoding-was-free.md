*Published 2026-08-06; updated 2026-08-10 with the completed Qwen3.6-27B pair.
Rakuen builds aimee, the system measured here. Every figure traces to the
[evidence repository](https://github.com/RakuenSoftware/rakuen-blog/tree/main/articles/speculative-decoding-was-free),
with a per-figure provenance map in
[evidence/figures.md](https://github.com/RakuenSoftware/rakuen-blog/blob/main/articles/speculative-decoding-was-free/evidence/figures.md).*

A small model can guess several next tokens while the target model checks them in
one pass. llama.cpp calls this multi-token prediction (MTP). When the target keeps
a guess, generation skips work it would otherwise do one token at a time. F1
means the harmonic mean of precision and recall; it is the accuracy metric here.

We now have seven same-card pairs: six Gemma 4 configurations over 10,000 notes
each, plus Qwen3.6-27B over 1,001 notes. In every pair, MTP was the intended
difference. The result is consistent across both families: throughput rose
sharply, while extraction accuracy did not move enough to distinguish from zero.

## Seven pairs, no resolved accuracy change

The chart shows MTP-on minus MTP-off. A positive or negative dot is not, by
itself, a win or loss. The uncertainty range around every measured difference
crosses zero, so all seven accuracy results are ties at this experiment's
resolution.

The six Gemma differences range from −0.0022 to +0.0039. Three 10,000-note
bootstrap ranges stay within about half a percentage point of zero. Qwen's

In plain English: the scores wobble slightly in both directions, but the data do
not support claiming that MTP helped or hurt accuracy. The table's throughput
column is different: every pair became faster, from +84.0% to +134.9%.

Gemma keeps 78.5% to 82.0% of its guesses. Qwen keeps 79.04%: 806,937 of
1,020,888 proposed tokens. That places Qwen inside the Gemma range and confirms
that the Qwen speedup came from active drafting, not a label or timing mistake.

Acceptance does not determine the speedup by itself. Larger models can gain more
while accepting slightly fewer guesses because each avoided memory read costs
more.

## Qwen has two separate speed levers

Before the paired MTP test, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B produced 234 words/s without MTP,
versus 67.8 for dense Qwen3.6-27B on a comparable RTX 5090. That is an architecture
result: the sparse mixture-of-experts model activates only part of its weights per
token. Neither run used MTP.

A smaller model leaves less idle capacity for MTP to reclaim. High concurrency
can consume the same capacity: at 32 simultaneous requests, MTP was slightly
slower in the Gemma test. Treat those as separate operating modes.

The practical rule is:

- Compare MTP on and off on the same card, model, notes and concurrency.
- Record the server's proposed and accepted token counters.
- Report both throughput and a paired accuracy range.
- Call a result a tie when that range crosses zero; do not rank tiny point
  differences as wins.

The completed Qwen3.6-27B pair closes the second-family gap and agrees with the
six Gemma pairs. The Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MTP comparison is still incomplete and is
excluded. The available Qwen pair supports the operational conclusion: enable
MTP for this workload, then measure the exact gain on the system that will run it.
